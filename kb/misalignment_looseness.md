## Symptoms of misalignment

When the motor shaft and the driven shaft are not in one line, the coupling forces both shafts to bend a
little on every turn. The sound gains a strong low tone at once or twice the running speed, the coupling
rubber wears and sheds dust, and the bearings on both sides run warm. Misalignment often appears right after a
motor was removed and refitted, or after the base bolts settled.

## Symptoms of looseness

Loose foundation bolts, a cracked base, or a bearing loose in its housing make the machine rattle. The sound
has many harmonics and changes when someone presses on the base or when the load changes. The owner may hear
a knock at start-up or shut-down. A soft foot (one motor foot not sitting flat) feels like looseness and also
causes misalignment.

## Hand check (machine off)

With the machine off and locked out: check that every base and motor bolt is tight, and look for cracks in the
base or the grout. Look at the coupling: worn or torn rubber, rust powder, or a gap that is wider on one side
all point to misalignment. A straight edge laid across the two coupling halves should sit flat on both.

## What the mechanic does

The mechanic tightens or replaces the bolts, shims a soft foot, and aligns the shafts with a dial gauge or a
laser tool. The coupling element is replaced if it is worn. Alignment is checked again after the bolts are
torqued, because tightening can move the motor. The machine's sound is recorded after the job to verify it.

## Prevention

Check alignment every time a motor or pump is refitted, and after any work on the base. Re-check bolts after
the first weeks of a new installation, when foundations settle.
