## Symptoms

A worn rolling bearing usually announces itself weeks before it fails. Early on the change is only in the
high-pitched part of the sound: a faint hiss, rumble or ticking on top of the normal hum, which an owner may
describe as "khat-khat", "ghar-ghar" or "kirr-kirr". Later the housing runs warmer than usual, the noise is
heard from a distance, and vibration can be felt on the motor body. A bearing that is close to seizing can
make a grinding or screeching sound and the motor may draw more current and trip.

## What the sound shows

On a spectrogram, bearing damage shows first as energy rising in the high bands compared with the machine's
own normal recording, often as short repeating impacts. Inner-race, outer-race and ball defects repeat at
different multiples of the running speed, which is why a vibration sensor can tell them apart while a phone
microphone usually cannot. A steady rise over several days matters more than one loud reading.

## Hand check (machine off)

Only after the machine is switched off and locked out by the person doing the check: touch the bearing
housing at each end of the motor. A housing that is too hot to keep a hand on, compared with the other end
or with a similar healthy machine, points to the bearing. Turn the shaft slowly by hand: roughness, a
gritty feel, or play when the shaft is lifted means the bearing is worn. Look for grease leaking or dried,
darkened grease around the seal.

## What the mechanic does

The mechanic removes the coupling or pulley, pulls the old bearing with a puller, and fits a new bearing of
the same number as the old one (the number is printed on its side). Bearings are fitted by pressing on the
inner ring or by heating the bearing, never by hammering the outer ring. The shaft seat and the housing are
checked: if either is scored or worn oval, a new bearing will fail again and the shaft or end shield needs
repair. After fitting, the motor is run and its sound recorded so the repair can be verified against the
machine's own normal.

## Prevention

Grease at the interval the motor maker gives, with the grease type they name, and not too much: over-greasing
also heats a bearing. Keep dust and water out of the housing. Check belt tension and coupling alignment after
any refit, because a tight belt or a misaligned coupling loads the bearing. Do not overload the machine.

## If it is ignored

A bearing that runs to destruction can let the rotor rub the stator, which overheats and can burn the
winding. Then the job becomes a rewind instead of a bearing change, with far more downtime.
