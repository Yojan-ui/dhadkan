## Symptoms

A motor with a winding problem hums louder than normal, runs hot, smells of burnt varnish, trips the starter
or blows fuses, and may be slow to come up to speed. The sound often shows a stronger tone at twice the supply
frequency. Common causes: single-phasing (one of three phases lost), low or unbalanced supply voltage,
overload, a blocked cooling fan or fins covered in dust, water getting in, and a bearing failure that let the
rotor rub.

## Hand check (machine off)

With the supply switched off and locked out: look at the cooling fins and the fan cover for dust and blockage,
and smell for burnt insulation. Look at the terminal box for loose, burnt or discoloured connections. Electrical
tests (insulation resistance with a megger, winding resistance, the current in each phase) are for a
qualified electrician only.

## What the mechanic does

An electrician first checks the supply: all three phases present and balanced, the voltage within range, and
the overload relay set to the motor's rated current. A winding that has failed or whose insulation reads low is
rewound at a rewinding shop, where the bearings are usually replaced too. After a rewind, a no-load and a load
test are done and the motor's sound is recorded as its new normal.

## Prevention

Keep the cooling fins and fan clean, protect the motor against single-phasing with a proper relay, do not run
it above its rated load, and keep water away from the terminal box.

## Warranty

Repair shops usually void a rewinding warranty when the motor failed again because of wrong supply voltage,
single-phasing, phase reversal or mechanical damage. Keep the repair bill and the verified repair record.
