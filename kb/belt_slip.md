## Symptoms

A slipping V-belt squeals, most of all at start-up or when the load comes on, and the driven machine runs
slower than it should. Owners describe it as "chee-chee" or a whistle. The belt gets hot and shiny on its
sides (glazed), and black rubber dust collects under the guard. A belt that is about to break shows cracks
across its back.

## Hand check (machine off)

With the machine off and locked out and the guard removed by someone trained to do it: press the belt in the
middle of its longest span. It should move about the thickness of a finger, roughly 1 to 2 cm per 100 cm of
span; much more means it is loose. Look for cracks, glazing and frayed edges. Lay a straight edge across the
two pulley faces: they should be in one line. Look into the pulley grooves for wear: a belt that sits deep in
the groove means the pulley or the belt is worn.

## What the mechanic does

The mechanic sets the tension with the motor's adjusting bolts, and replaces a glazed, cracked or stretched
belt. On a drive with several belts, all of them are replaced together as a matched set, because a new belt
next to old ones carries the whole load. The pulleys are aligned. A new belt is re-tensioned after the first
day of running, because it stretches.

## Prevention

Check tension every few weeks, keep oil and grease off the belts, and keep the guard on. Use the belt section
the drive was designed for.
