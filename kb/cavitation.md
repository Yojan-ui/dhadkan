## Symptoms

A cavitating pump sounds as if it is pumping gravel: a crackling, rattling noise from the pump casing, with
the delivery pressure and flow lower and unsteady. It happens when the pressure at the pump inlet is too low,
so bubbles form and collapse inside the impeller. Common causes in small factories: a clogged foot valve or
strainer, a suction pipe that is too long or too thin, a water level that dropped, an air leak on the suction
side, or a delivery valve opened too far.

## Hand check

With the pump off and locked out: clean the strainer and the foot valve, check the water level in the sump or
tank, and look for loose joints on the suction pipe (air leaks often show as a hiss or as drips when the pump
is off). When the pump is running, an owner can listen from a safe distance: the crackle often reduces when
the delivery valve is closed a little.

## What the mechanic does

The mechanic clears the suction, fixes air leaks, and checks the suction lift and pipe size against the pump.
If the impeller is already pitted, it is replaced. A leaking mechanical seal, often damaged by running dry or
by cavitation, is replaced at the same visit.

## Prevention

Clean strainers on a schedule, never run the pump dry, keep the suction short and of the pipe size the pump
needs, and keep the water level above the minimum the pump maker gives.

## If it is ignored

Cavitation eats the impeller surface and the shocks damage the seal and the bearings. A pump that runs dry
for long can burn its seal within minutes.
