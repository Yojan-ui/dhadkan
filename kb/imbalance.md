## Symptoms

An unbalanced rotor or impeller shakes the machine once per revolution. The sound gains a steady low drone at
the running speed and the vibration is strongest across the shaft, in the radial direction. In fans the usual
causes are dust or flour built up unevenly on the blades, a bent or broken blade, or a lost balance weight. In
pumps, a worn or partly clogged impeller does the same.

## Hand check (machine off)

With the fan or pump off and locked out: look at the blades or impeller for build-up, cracks, bends and
missing pieces. Spin it slowly by hand and watch whether it always stops in the same position: a heavy spot
tends to settle at the bottom. Check that the hub is tight on the shaft.

## What the mechanic does

First the blades or impeller are cleaned, which often removes most of the imbalance. Damaged blades are
replaced. If it still shakes, the rotor is dynamically balanced, in a workshop or on site, by adding or
removing small weights. After balancing, the sound is recorded and compared with the machine's own normal.

## Prevention

Clean fan blades on a schedule, especially where flour, cotton fluff or dust is in the air. Never run a fan
with a damaged blade. Keep the suction strainer of a pump clean so that debris does not reach the impeller.

## If it is ignored

Imbalance loads the bearings on every turn, so they wear out early, and the shaking loosens bolts and can crack
the base or the fan housing.
